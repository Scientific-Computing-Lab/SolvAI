\subsection*{Testing transfer to larger molecules}



We separate accuracy on labelled larger molecules from the shape of predictions
for still larger, unlabelled series. For the first test, we held out the largest
15\% within each endpoint-label source and refitted eight fixed configurations on
1,160 remaining labels. Of the 205 held-out molecules, 188 exceed the largest
training molecule's 17 heavy atoms. Their labels had been available in earlier
random-fold development, so this is an exploratory test of endpoint size transfer
with the source surrogates held fixed, not an independent test of the whole
pipeline beyond all prior source exposure.

On this held-out split, TabPFN lowers MAE from 1.772 for ExtraTrees to 1.318
kcal mol$^{-1}$ (paired difference $-0.453$; 95\% interval, $-0.648$ to $-0.281$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:size}a). The responses also help TabPFN itself: its matched error
without them is 1.959 (response-minus-control interval, $-0.847$ to $-0.451$).
Several other neural endpoints improve on ExtraTrees here as well.
They are useful alternatives for this split, although their separately
selected input recipes mean the cross-endpoint difference is not solely an
architecture effect (Supplementary Table~15 and Data~6).

In those unlabelled series, ExtraTrees predictions approach a
plateau, while several neural endpoints and TabPFN keep changing as residues
are added (Fig.~\ref{fig:size}b,c). That shape is less constrained by the fitted
range, but it cannot be scored as more accurate without reference values.
An alkane series makes the point from another direction: TabPFN is non-monotonic
between C10 and C14, and most tested alkanes already occur in endpoint training.
The individual predictions, source-label audit and sensitivity to two alkane
labels are in Supplementary Methods~S20 and Data~6.

Predictive uncertainty adds a final check on using this endpoint for new
molecules. TabPFN's nominal 80\% and 95\% predictive intervals cover only
58.2\% and 80.0\% of External-220, respectively, with similar undercoverage
on Strict-97 (Supplementary Fig.~11 and Table~16). These native intervals have
not been calibrated on the external labels; their widths should not yet be
read as reliable per-molecule error guarantees.
