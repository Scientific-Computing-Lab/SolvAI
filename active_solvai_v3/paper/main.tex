\title{Do structure-predicted solvent responses identify where alchemical simulations need more sampling?}
\author{Gal Oren \and Michael Levitt}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Alchemical free-energy calculations distribute simulation effort across a set of
intermediate Hamiltonians. Equal allocation is simple, but inefficient when sampling
difficulty varies strongly between windows. We asked whether the fifteen
structure-predicted solvent-response coordinates in SolvAI could identify difficult
windows before or during a calculation, beyond diagnostics derived from the target
trajectory itself. We preserved the fixed 15-point integration grid and preregistered
a molecule-held-out, zero-new-simulation identifiability gate before considering new
physics. Across twelve molecules and 180 PIMD2 windows, early variance diagnostics
modestly predicted disagreement between later complementary blocks. A generic
observed-data model reduced log-difficulty error from 1.547 to \GateGenericMAE, but the
SolvAI-conditioned model increased it to \GateSolvAIMAE\ and was indistinguishable from
molecule-shuffled controls. The SolvAI increment was absent at every fixed prefix
between 0.5 and 3 ps. Independent-reference planning further showed that the first
predeclared design with adequate power and reference precision would require 256
molecules, two full-grid streams and 3 ns per stream-window, approximately
\MinimumDesignReservedGPU
reserved RTX 3090 GPU-hours under optimistic noise scaling. No new simulation was
therefore launched. The result separates two forms of transferable physics: response
coordinates that improve an experimental hydration endpoint need not predict the
sampling difficulty of the underlying alchemical dynamics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Adaptive simulation is attractive because different alchemical windows can have very
different correlation times, transients and estimator variances. An allocator that
recognizes those differences early could devote additional trajectory time where it
most reduces uncertainty while retaining a conservative integration grid. The key
question for Active SolvAI is stricter than whether adaptive sampling works: molecular
response information predicted before simulation must improve allocation beyond a
competent policy that already observes the target trajectory.

Earlier Active SolvAI studies addressed a different allocation variable. They tested
molecule-specific selection of lambda locations and found that a SolvAI-conditioned
Bayesian-quadrature policy lost to uniform direct integration. An apparently strong
same-curve oracle also collapsed when lambda positions were selected and evaluated on
complementary trajectory blocks. A proposed independent-replica resolution experiment
was stopped at its prospective power gate. Those negative results rule out a simple
return to placement optimization, but do not establish whether all fixed windows need
equal trajectory length.

We therefore isolated a new estimand: all policies use the same Hamiltonian, fifteen
lambda values, quadrature and mandatory initial sampling floor; only the allocation of
additional immutable trajectory chunks may differ. Before spending new GPU time, we
asked whether the requisite target---future window-level sampling difficulty---is
identifiable from existing trajectory prefixes, and whether frozen SolvAI responses
add information beyond generic observed diagnostics. This preregistered gate produced
a negative answer for the SolvAI increment and a prohibitive lower-bound design for a
definitive independent test.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Inherited evidence fixes the test}

All parent SolvAI and Active SolvAI results were regenerated from machine-readable
artifacts. The parent response layer improves the matched experimental hydration model
from 0.303 to 0.202 kcal mol$^{-1}$ on the ARROW reference set, but the prior Active
program found no endpoint gain from actual three-point PIMD2 responses. On eight dense
sentinels, Active Bayesian quadrature produced integral MAEs of 1.701 and 1.608 kcal
mol$^{-1}$ at five and seven windows, compared with 1.153 and 1.092 kcal mol$^{-1}$
for uniform direct integration. Cross-block oracle selection was worse than uniform by
0.431 and 0.409 kcal mol$^{-1}$. These results were retained without reinterpretation.

The trajectory census found 363 qualified PIMD2 response files, including 72 molecules
with complete observations at lambda 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9. Only twelve molecules had all
fifteen windows, and each molecule-window had one 5-ps stream. Contiguous portions of
these trajectories can diagnose identifiability but cannot provide independent-replica
validation.

\subsection{Generic diagnostics carry modest signal}

For each of the 180 dense molecule-windows, the preregistered primary analysis exposed
the first 1 ps and predicted a stabilized measure of disagreement between two
non-overlapping later 1-ps blocks. All preprocessing and ridge-penalty selection were
nested within leave-one-molecule-out validation. The generic model used lambda,
quadrature weight and prefix diagnostics; the matched SolvAI-conditioned model added
the fifteen frozen response coordinates and fixed lambda interactions.

The lambda/protocol baseline had a log-difficulty MAE of 1.547. Generic trajectory
diagnostics lowered this to 1.427, a paired difference of -0.120 (90\% confidence
interval, -0.199 to -0.041). The 7.7\% reduction was statistically directional but
missed the preregistered 10\% materiality threshold. Variance and SEM-like statistics
were the most informative individual diagnostics, with rank correlations of
approximately 0.32--0.36 with later complementary disagreement.

\subsection{SolvAI conditioning does not improve allocation information}

The SolvAI-conditioned model had an MAE of \GateSolvAIMAE, \GateSolvAIDifference\ higher
than the generic model (paired 90\% confidence interval, \GateSolvAILow\ to
\GateSolvAIHigh), with six of twelve molecules
improved (Fig.~\ref{fig:gate1}). Mean within-molecule rank correlation was 0.225,
below the frozen 0.30 criterion. The aligned model differed from the mean of five
molecule-shuffled controls by only -0.0102 (90\% confidence interval, -0.0478 to
0.0310). SolvAI conditioning was also worse than generic diagnostics at 0.5, 2 and
3 ps. SolvAI coordinates alone performed worse than lambda/protocol by 0.129 (90\%
confidence interval, 0.051 to 0.225).

These controls distinguish a failure of the proposed molecule-conditioned increment
from a total absence of time-series information. Generic diagnostics had modest value;
the frozen response representation did not identify additional window-specific
difficulty.

\subsection{A resolving independent design is impractical}

We next ran a separately frozen simulation-based power and reference-reliability
calculation rather than launching a small campaign. The minimum-useful alternative was
a 20\% error reduction over the inherited generic policy scale. Molecule-level effect
heterogeneity came from the eight development-exposed v1 cases, and independent
reference disagreement was propagated from the v2 complementary-block audit using
optimistic inverse-square-root time scaling.

The first adequate point in the predeclared grid used \MinimumDesignMolecules\ molecules,
fifteen windows, two independent streams and \MinimumDesignDuration\ ps per
stream-window (Fig.~\ref{fig:power}). This is 23.04 microseconds of production,
projected as \MinimumDesignProductionGPU\ RTX 3090 GPU-hours or \MinimumDesignReservedGPU
reserved hours including contingency. A 20-molecule, 50-ps campaign would have powers
of only 0.326 and 0.142 at the two fixed budgets and zero probability of passing the
reference criterion. No new trajectory was launched.

\section{Discussion}

This bounded study rejects the current Active SolvAI v3 hypothesis at its first causal
gate. SolvAI response coordinates are useful for the experimentally supervised
hydration endpoint, but that usefulness does not transfer automatically to sampling
difficulty. The two targets depend on different information: endpoint response priors
describe solvent thermodynamics, whereas effort allocation depends on correlation,
relaxation and finite-time estimator behavior under a particular simulation protocol.

The generic result is informative but insufficient for a new claim. Early variance
statistics modestly anticipate later disagreement, suggesting that ordinary adaptive
sampling could be useful with long independent development trajectories. Yet generic
adaptation alone is not Active SolvAI, and it missed the frozen materiality threshold
on the inherited short data. The correct conclusion is therefore a no-go for the
present molecule-conditioned formulation, not evidence that adaptive sampling is
impossible.

The main limitation is measurement. One 5-ps stream cannot establish an independent
sampling-difficulty target, and inverse-square-root extrapolation may be optimistic in
the presence of slow modes. That limitation does not justify an underpowered pilot:
the frozen planning calculation makes the required scale explicit. A future program
would need a response representation trained specifically on protocol-level sampling
difficulty across hundreds of independent full-grid trajectories. It would be a new
research program rather than a continuation or rescue of the present test.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Prospective governance}

The v3 blueprint was hashed and read in full. The inherited evidence audit and master
protocol were committed before Gate-1 scoring; a pre-result amendment clarified fixed
lambda interactions needed for molecule-conditioned linear predictions. The power and
reference protocol was committed after Gate 1 but before its Monte Carlo calculation.
All prior molecules and results were marked development-exposed. No experimental
hydration label entered a v3 predictor, policy, stopping criterion or selection rule.

\subsection{Trajectory processing}

We read native Arbalest `SYSTEM dHdL` values in kcal mol$^{-1}$, removed the time-zero
record and retained 50 observations at 0.1-ps intervals per window. Lambda values were
ordered numerically and integrated with the fixed trapezoidal weights. Each stream was
partitioned into ten 0.5-ps blocks. The primary prefix used frames through 1.0 ps; the
target was
\[
\log\left[\tfrac{1}{2}(\bar y_{3.1:4.0}-\bar y_{4.1:5.0})^2+0.25^2\right].
\]
Sensitivity analyses used 0.5-, 2- and 3-ps prefixes and stabilizers of 0.10 and
0.50 kcal mol$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Diagnostics and response features}

Prefix summaries included the response mean and variance, naive SEM,
non-overlapping and overlapping batch variance rates, a Bartlett--Newey--West variance
rate, lag-1 autocorrelation, initial-positive-sequence autocorrelation time, effective
sample size, half-prefix location and scale differences, Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance,
linear/rank drift, temperature, density and unresolved-equilibration flags. SolvAI
coordinates were generated by the immutable released structure-to-response surrogates.
No source calculation was rerun.

\subsection{Nested prediction and controls}

Ridge penalties 0.1, 1, 10, 100 and 1,000 were selected inside each outer training set
using leave-one-training-molecule-out error. Imputation and standardization were fit on
training rows only. Molecule-level coordinates entered as main effects and fixed
interactions with centered lambda and its square. Five joint molecule-level response
permutations used seeds 88031--88035. Primary uncertainty used 100,000 molecule-
clustered bootstrap resamples with seed 20260903.

\subsection{Power and cost}

The power calculation ran 50,000 Monte Carlo trials per condition. It centered the
eight inherited paired policy effects and added null, 20\% and 30\% alternatives.
Detection required a two-sided 95\% paired interval excluding zero. Reference
disagreements were resampled from the 24 unique 2.5-ps complementary partitions and
scaled by the square root of inverse duration. Production cost used the measured
369.902 PIMD2 ps GPU$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$ throughput; the operational reservation added
11\% for failures and restarts.

\section*{Data and code availability}

The branch `active-solvai-v3-effort-allocation` contains every freeze, raw-file hash,
molecule-level prediction, decision ledger, analysis script and compiled report.
Earlier SolvAI and Active SolvAI histories remain immutable. No new simulation data
were generated in v3.
